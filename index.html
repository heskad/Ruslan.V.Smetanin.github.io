[ 02 / WHO I AM ]

From Yakutia to Philadelphia

A nonlinear path
into science.

My journey began in a remote village in Yakutia. Curiosity, persistence and a willingness to change direction carried me from agricultural biology to molecular biology, bioinformatics and cancer research.

WET LAB ↔ CODECANCER BIOLOGYBIOINFORMATICSSCIENTIFIC COMPUTING

THE STORY SO FAR

I began my research career by growing and studying mutant forms of hop-like alfalfa. During my master's training at Alferov University, I moved closer to the molecular scale, working on the discovery and characterization of BRICHOS antimicrobial peptides.

A delayed move to the United States led me to PhD studies at Moscow State University. I completed coursework and research training in bioinformatics from 2023 to 2026, then left the program before completing the degree. The experience still became an important bridge between my experimental background and computational biology.

Today I work at Fox Chase Cancer Center in Philadelphia under the supervision of Dr. Vladimir Kolenko. My research focuses on kidney and prostate cancer and combines molecular experiments, cancer cell models and data analysis.

A scientific career does not have to follow a straight line to move forward.

THE NEXT CHAPTER

Make wet-lab biology and computation speak the same language.

My plan is to deepen my work in cancer biology while developing stronger bioinformatics and scientific-computing skills. I want to build reproducible projects in which experiments and data analysis guide each other, and eventually help other young scientists see that an unconventional path can still become a meaningful scientific career.
